Before You Go

The Essentials

Everything you need before leaving the hotel.

Get a Navigo Easy card at any Métro stationCovers Métro, RER, bus, funicular, and Versailles. Use the Bonjour RATP app to navigate.
Download Paris offline on Google Maps before you arriveSignal underground is unreliable. Download the area before you travel.
Uber works well — taxis are metered and reliableBoth are good options. Taxis from CDG airport are fixed fare.
Currency is Euro (€) — cards accepted almost everywhereKeep €20–30 cash for markets, small bakeries, and the occasional cash-only café.
Tipping is not obligatory in FranceService is included. Rounding up is appreciated but never expected.
Museums: book timed entry online — alwaysLouvre closed Tuesdays. Musée d'Orsay closed Mondays. Versailles closed Mondays.
Paris in spring and autumn is idealSummer is hot and crowded. Winter is cold but atmospheric. Layers always.
Comfortable shoes are non-negotiableParis is a walking city. Cobblestones in the Marais are unforgiving.
Wallace Fountains — free cold drinking water everywhereOver 100 dark-green fountains across Paris. No need to buy bottled water.

The world's largest art museum. Go straight to the Denon Wing: Winged Victory, Venus de Milo, the Mona Lisa. That alone is three hours.

Palace — Hall of Mirrors
Do not miss ▾

Take the RER C. Leave Paris by 9am. The Hall of Mirrors — 73 metres of gilded arches and 357 mirrors — is genuinely difficult to comprehend until you are inside.

TipBook Palace tickets online before you travel. The on-the-day queue can exceed two hours.

Insider Tips

Good to Know

The things guidebooks either miss or refuse to say.

Always say Bonjour when entering any shop, café, or restaurantNot saying it is considered genuinely rude. Also Merci when you leave. These two words change every interaction.
Do not eat immediately next to the landmarksRestaurants next to major landmarks are almost universally mediocre. Walk two streets in any direction.
Book everything in advance — not optionalLouvre, Eiffel Tower, Versailles, Sainte-Chapelle, Notre-Dame. Queues without tickets can eat half a day.
Paris is a walking city — use itMany Métro stops are closer than they look. Walking along the Seine is often faster and always better.
The Galeries Lafayette rooftop is completely freeBoulevard Haussmann. Open access, stunning panorama over the Opéra and Eiffel Tower. No ticket, no queue.
Look for the chalkboard menu — it means seasonal and freshA laminated menu near a tourist site is a red flag. A chalkboard on the pavement means the chef cares.
Lunch is the better meal for valueMost Paris bistros offer a fixed-price lunch formule that costs a third of what the same dishes cost at dinner.
Café culture: stand at the bar for cheaper coffeeThe same espresso costs less at the bar than at a table. The French stand at the bar — join them.
Never skip the Bonjour — everIn Paris, omitting the greeting is the fastest way to receive poor service and a cold response.
Never try to cross the Arc de Triomphe roundabout on footUse the underground pedestrian crossing. Twelve converging lanes, no pedestrian right of way.
Never exchange money at airport bureaux de changeThe rates at CDG are extremely poor. Use a city ATM or bring a Wise/Revolut card.
