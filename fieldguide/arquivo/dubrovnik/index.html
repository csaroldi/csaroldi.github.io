A Field Guide to

Dubrovnik

by Claudio SAROLDI

Beautiful, overcrowded, and completely unapologetic about both.

↓
Before You Go

The Essentials

The practical layer. Read this before anything else.

Best length4 days works. 5 days feels much better and less rushed. Anything under 3 days and you are just skimming.
Best monthsMay, early June, September, early October. July and August are genuinely brutal — heat, crowds, and cruise ships all peak at once.
Golden ruleDo the City Walls and heavy walking early morning or after 5pm. Midday Dubrovnik is a stone oven with no shade and a crowd problem.
Cruise ship daysOn a bad day, three ships dock and 12,000 passengers flood the Old Town before 10am. Google 'Dubrovnik port schedule', check your dates, and plan around the heavy days.
Old TownEntirely on foot. Limestone streets get slippery when wet, especially on steps. Wear proper shoes — not flip flops.
City busLine 6 runs between Pile Gate and Lapad / Babin Kuk. Cheap, reliable, and how locals actually move.
Taxis and UberUber works well in Dubrovnik. Taxis are fine but agree on the price before you get in for longer runs.
CurrencyEuro since January 2023. Cards accepted everywhere. Still worth carrying some cash for smaller restaurants and markets.
City Walls ticketBuy online in advance to skip the queue. The entrance price increases every year — check the current rate before you go.
Dress codeShoulders and knees covered for churches and the Franciscan Monastery. The city is relaxed on the street but the sites will turn you away at the door.
AccommodationInside the Old Town walls is atmospheric but expensive and loud at night. Lapad and Babin Kuk give better value — bus ride in is 10 minutes.
PhotographyEarly morning and golden hour. The Stradun at 7am is one of the best shots in Europe. At noon it is just a crowd.
Day by Day

The Itinerary

Five days structured so each one has a different energy. Skip Day 5 if you only have four.

Pile Gate
Start here▾
HistoryThe main western entrance to the Old Town. The outer gate dates to 1537, the inner Gothic gate to the 14th century. The moat between them was never filled with water — Dubrovnik dug it dry to slow attackers, not flood them.
Why it mattersBest clean starting point before the streets fill up. The statue above the inner gate is St Blaise, the city's patron — you will see him on nearly every building.
📍 Take me there
Stradun
The main act▾
HistoryOriginally a shallow sea channel separating two settlements, filled in during the 11th or 12th century. The uniform baroque appearance came after the catastrophic 1667 earthquake that killed around 5,000 people. The Republic rebuilt deliberately — standardising facades so no family could display wealth through their building.
Did you knowThe limestone has been polished to a mirror shine by centuries of feet. Genuinely slippery when wet. Early morning, with no one on it, it is one of the best streets in Europe.
📍 Take me there
Franciscan Monastery
Do not miss▾
HistoryThe monastery pharmacy was founded in 1317 and has been operating continuously ever since — one of the oldest working pharmacies in the world. The Romanesque-Gothic cloister survived the 1667 earthquake largely intact, which is remarkable given how much of the city did not.
Did you knowThe pharmacy still sells its own cosmetic products based on old recipes. You can buy face cream from a medieval dispensary. Not many places can claim that.
📍 Take me there
Rector's Palace
▾
HistoryThe seat of the Rector of the Republic of Ragusa — an independent city-state from the 7th century to 1806. The Rector served a one-month term, lived in the Palace during that month, and could not leave without permission. His own family could not visit him. It was a deliberate system to stop any one person accumulating power.
Why stopThis is where Dubrovnik stops being a pretty film set and becomes a serious old republic. The system they built to control their own leaders was more sophisticated than most modern democracies.
📍 Take me there
Jesuit Stairs
Photo stop▾
HistoryBuilt in the early 18th century to connect Gundulić Square with the Jesuit Church of St Ignatius. Designed by Pietro Passalacqua — the same architect later involved in Rome's Spanish Steps, which explains why the two look related.
RealityYes, people will pose dramatically here. You will too. That is fine. Late afternoon light from the top is genuinely good.
📍 Take me there
City Walls
Critical▾
HistoryAmong the most complete medieval city walls in Europe: 1,940 metres long, up to 6 metres thick on the landward side, 3 circular and 12 rectangular towers. Built mostly in the 14th and 15th centuries, significantly reinforced as the Ottoman threat grew. They were never breached by a foreign army.
Best moveEarly (gates open at 8am) or after 5pm only. Midday on an exposed limestone walkway with no shade is miserable. Go anticlockwise — you get the better sea views first.
📍 Take me there
Porporela
Sunset▾
HistoryThe old harbour breakwater on the eastern side of the Old Town. In the Republic of Ragusa era it was a working port structure. Today it is where locals sit in the evening while tourists are busy photographing the Stradun.
Why it mattersLess formal, more genuine, and the right way to end Day 1 watching the light drop over the Adriatic.
📍 Take me there

Mount Srđ
Big view▾
HistoryThe 412-metre peak directly behind the Old Town. The French-built Fort Imperial at the summit saw fierce fighting during the 1991–92 siege of Dubrovnik, when Yugoslav and Montenegrin forces shelled the city from the surrounding hills. It is now a Homeland War Museum — a conflict most visitors walk past without knowing about.
Best moveCable car is fast and worth it for the view. Hike up if you want to earn it. Do not skip the war museum — it completely changes how you read the city.
📍 Take me there
Fort Lovrijenac
Must see▾
HistoryBuilt in the 11th century on a 37-metre cliff outside the western walls — specifically to stop the Venetians from building a fort there first. The Republic reportedly built it in three months. Above the entrance gate is inscribed: Non bene pro toto libertas venditur auro — 'Freedom is not sold for all the gold in the world.'
Why it mattersThe opposite angle on the city from the sea side. From here Dubrovnik looks militarily serious, not just photogenic. The view from the top terrace is one of the best in the city.
📍 Take me there
Buža side passages
Quieter▾
HistoryThe southern edge of the Old Town, where the walls meet the cliff above the sea. Buža means 'hole in the wall' — a small gate cut through the city walls gives access to rocky platforms below, and to the famous cliff bar of the same name.
Why stopBetter sea air, more genuine atmosphere. The passages behind the main wall are markedly quieter than the Stradun side. Good drink with a view if you time it right.
📍 Take me there
Lazareti
Hidden gem▾
HistoryIn 1377, the Republic of Ragusa introduced the world's first formal quarantine law. Arrivals from plague-affected areas waited 30 days before entering — later extended to 40, quarantina in Italian, which is where the word 'quarantine' comes from. The Lazareti complex, built in the early 17th century outside the Ploče Gate, was the physical infrastructure for this system.
Did you knowRagusa got rich partly by isolating disease without shutting down trade. Merchants and goods were held separately so commerce could continue. A 14th-century public health solution that still makes sense.
📍 Take me there

Boat from the Old Port
Easy win▾
Getting thereSmall ferries run every 30–45 minutes from the Old Port. Crossing takes about 15 minutes. Check the last return time before you lose track of the hours — boats stop in the evening.
Why it worksIn 15 minutes you go from the most photographed city in Europe to a nature reserve with peacocks. Minimum logistics, maximum reset.
📍 Take me there
Benedictine Monastery ruins
▾
HistoryFounded in 1023. Richard the Lionheart reportedly sheltered here in 1192 after being shipwrecked on his way back from the Third Crusade, and vowed to build a church — the city of Ragusa redirected the funds to the Cathedral instead. The monastery was dissolved by Napoleon's administration in 1798 and has been a romantic ruin ever since.
Why stopIt makes Lokrum feel like more than a swim break. The cloister garden in the ruins is unexpectedly beautiful.
📍 Take me there
Fort Royal
Best view▾
HistoryBuilt by the French during Napoleon's occupation of Dubrovnik (1806–1813) as a lookout over the Adriatic approaches. The French period was brief but left several military structures on the islands and hills around the city.
Best moveDo this before the swim stop while your legs still cooperate. The view back to the Old Town and across the Elaphiti Islands is the best on Lokrum.
📍 Take me there
The Dead Sea
▾
What it isA small saltwater lake connected to the Adriatic by an underground passage. Similar salinity to the sea but noticeably warmer, surrounded by pine trees, completely calm. Unlike anything else on the island.
RealityIt is the kind of place where people lose track of time very easily. Factor that into your last boat calculation.
📍 Take me there

Elaphiti Islands — overview
Best day trip▾
HistoryAn archipelago of 13 islands (only 3 inhabited) northwest of Dubrovnik. The name comes from the Greek elaphos, meaning deer — though the deer are long gone. In the Republic of Ragusa era, the islands supplied timber and agricultural goods to the city.
Why it winsBest reward-to-hassle ratio of any full day out of Dubrovnik. No border crossings, no long drives, very good swimming, completely different pace.
📍 Take me there
Lopud — the classic choice
Crowd pleaser▾
CharacterThe most visited of the three islands and with good reason. Šunj beach on the south side is one of the few sandy beaches near Dubrovnik — a 20-minute walk or short buggy ride from the village. The village has some of the best-preserved Renaissance summer residences of the old Ragusan nobility.
Best forThe classic full island day. Easy, rewarding, good swimming, proper holiday feel.
Koločep — the quieter option
Calmer▾
CharacterSmallest and nearest of the three inhabited islands — 30 minutes from Dubrovnik. No cars allowed. Two small villages connected by a pine forest path. Good for a gentle swim morning before continuing.
Best forA lighter, slower island leg with clear water and no crowds.
Šipan — the hidden choice
Least touristed▾
CharacterThe largest of the three islands and the least touristed. Two villages connected by a fertile valley unusual for this part of the Adriatic. Several abandoned Ragusan noble villas in various states of romantic decay.
Best forGenuine island atmosphere without chasing the obvious option. You will likely share the ferry with locals, not just tourists.

Back in Dubrovnik
Go with a proven winner. Bistro Tavulin or Kopun — not a random waterfront gamble.
Option A — Cavtat20km south. Pretty, easy, lower stress. Built on the site of Epidaurum, the ancient city whose survivors fled to found Ragusa. The Račić Mausoleum by Ivan Meštrović on the hill is unexpectedly excellent.
Option B — Trsteno20km northwest. A Renaissance arboretum established in 1492 by a Ragusan noble family — one of the oldest in Europe. Very good antidote to stone and crowd overload. Game of Thrones filmed here too.
Option C — Ston60km northwest. Medieval walls stretching 5.5km across the Pelješac Peninsula — among the longest in Europe. Built by the Republic of Ragusa in the 14th century to protect its salt pans. Raw, less polished, historically serious.
Eat Well

Local Food

What is worth ordering and where. Not a giant spreadsheet of regret.

RozataDubrovnik's version of crème caramel, made with rose liqueur. The signature dessert of the city. Order it without hesitation.
Kopun (capon)Castrated rooster slow-cooked with vegetables and herbs. The traditional centrepiece of a Dubrovnik meal. Richer and more flavourful than regular chicken.
Zelena menestraA hearty green stew with smoked pork and kale. A very old Dalmatian dish that does not photograph well but tastes exactly right.
Sporchi makaruliPasta with a rich meat ragù. The name is worse than the dish. A Dubrovnik staple worth trying.
PekaMeat and vegetables slow-cooked under a bell-shaped lid buried in embers. Needs to be ordered hours in advance. Worth planning for.
DingačCroatia's first protected designation of origin wine. A powerful, full-bodied red from Plavac Mali grapes grown on steep slopes of the Pelješac Peninsula. The benchmark red of the region.
PošipThe best white of the region. Dry, aromatic, medium-bodied — from the island of Korčula. Very good in warm weather.
PostupAnother Pelješac red from Plavac Mali. Slightly softer than Dingač but still bold. Worth trying alongside it.
Dubrovnik MalvasiaA local white with a long history in the region. Light, crisp, very easy to drink. Good house white if they serve it.

Practical

Good to Know

The things that save time and prevent small disasters.

Check the port scheduleGoogle 'Dubrovnik port schedule' before you go. On days with three large ships, the Old Town is overwhelmed before 10am. Plan your heavy sightseeing around the quiet days.
City Walls timingGates open at 8am. Being first in is not a small advantage — it is the difference between a beautiful walk and a miserable shuffle. Book online to skip the entrance queue.
Old Town early and lateBefore 9am or after 9pm the Stradun is a different place. The evening light on the limestone is extraordinary. The midday version of Dubrovnik is not the real one.
Dubrovnik is expensiveOne of the most expensive destinations in Croatia — and increasingly in Europe. Prices have risen dramatically since the Game of Thrones tourism wave. Budget generously and do not compare it to Split or Zagreb.
CurrencyEuro since January 2023. Cards accepted everywhere. Still worth carrying some cash for smaller restaurants and boats.
Dress codeShoulders and knees covered for churches and the Franciscan Monastery. The city will not stop you on the street but the sites will turn you away at the door.
The limestone is genuinely slipperyEspecially when wet. Sandals and smooth-soled shoes are a bad idea. People fall every day in peak season. Wear proper footwear.
There are a lot of stairsThe Old Town is built on a hillside. Walking it properly involves significant elevation change. Factor this in if you have mobility concerns or are travelling with young children and a buggy.
Waterfront restaurants are almost always a trapThe view is real. The food and value rarely are. The good restaurants in Dubrovnik are not the ones with the best spots on the harbour — they are the ones locals go back to.
1991 happened hereMost visitors walk the Old Town with no idea that 30 years ago it was shelled during the Croatian War of Independence. The war museum on Mount Srđ takes an hour and changes your whole reading of the place.
Honest Advice

The Things That Actually Matter

Opinionated. Specific to Dubrovnik. Not on most travel guides.

🌊 The cruise ships are the actual problemOn a bad day, 12,000 cruise passengers arrive before 10am. The Old Town has around 1,500 residents. Google the port schedule before you finalise your days — it is the most useful thing you can do in advance.
🏰 Dubrovnik was a real independent republic for over a thousand yearsThe Republic of Ragusa survived from around the 7th century to 1806 — longer than the United States has existed. It outlasted empires and built one of the most sophisticated diplomatic systems in European history. The city you are walking around is not a medieval theme park.
📺 Game of Thrones is both the best and worst thing that happened hereIt brought enormous money and enormous crowds. The filming locations are interesting if you know the show. But the 'King's Landing' branding has flattened the city's actual identity. Dubrovnik was extraordinary before HBO arrived.
⚔️ Go to the war museum on Mount SrđIn 1991 and 1992 Dubrovnik was shelled during the Croatian War of Independence. A UNESCO World Heritage Site was bombarded. The Homeland War Museum at Fort Imperial takes an hour and completely changes how you read the city.
💶 Budget for more than you thinkDubrovnik is one of the most in-demand destinations in Europe with a physically limited Old Town that cannot expand. Prices keep going up. Do not compare it to other Croatian cities — it is its own category.
🌙 The Old Town after 9pm is a completely different cityOnce the cruise passengers are back on their ships, the Stradun quiets down dramatically. The evening light on the stone is extraordinary. Plan at least one evening where you are just walking with no destination.
